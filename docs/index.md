# Root-Ascent Method

**Causal Ladder** — A disciplined framework for moving from concrete incidents to systemic root causes and actionable levers.

**Current version: 0.4** (+ 0.4.1 documentation clarity) · Created through intensive AI collaboration.

---

## Why it exists

Most analyses of complex socio-technical problems stay at the symptom level or drift into speculation. Root-Ascent forces a controlled climb — and a deliberate return to action:

**Concrete Incident → Patterns → System Architecture → Higher-Order Cause → Levers → Operational Strategy**

Version 0.4 requires a **case-specific intent**, a brief **saturation check** before ascending, and **counter-hypotheses** before locking a higher-order cause. The familiar framing “capability scales faster than governance” remains a strong *candidate* hypothesis — not an automatic default.

Broader reading of the project intent: a reusable **protocol against drift** so human and artificial intelligence can stay oriented and action-capable under acceleration. Details: [INTENT.md](https://github.com/IrsanAI/root-ascent-method/blob/main/INTENT.md).

## Domain-agnostic

Validated application areas in the public case base (see EXAMPLES) include among others:

- Surveillance & biometric systems  
- AI agents, evaluation incidents, open agent stacks  
- Synthetic media, election integrity, investment fraud  
- Innovation diffusion (e.g. heat pumps)  
- Biosecurity / dual-use screening  
- Grid costs & ratepayer protection  
- Digital identity (e.g. EUDI wallet rollout)  
- Satellite direct-to-device communications  
- Housing markets & algorithmic pricing  
- Transport safety / hazard-to-action coupling  
- Regional IT value architecture (seeded commercial, anonymized)  

## Who it is for

**Primary:** Investigative journalists, independent researchers, civil-society technologists, technology-policy analysts, advanced AI users.  
**Secondary:** Academic researchers (STS, security studies, public policy), digital-rights organizations, strategic government teams.  
**Tertiary:** Think tanks, OSINT practitioners, socio-technical risk professionals.  
**Experimental (actively invited):** Research labs, PhD groups, AI-agent developers, data-journalism units, civic technologists — test it, stress it, improve it.
