# Root-Ascent Method

**[English](METHOD.md)** | **[Deutsch](METHOD.de.md)**

---

## Overview

The **Root-Ascent Method** (also called the **Causal Ladder Method**) is a disciplined process for analyzing complex socio-technical problems. It systematically moves from a concrete starting point to deeper structural causes and then back down to actionable levers.

## The Causal Ladder

1. **Concrete Incident** – A specific event, article, statement, or observation  
2. **Patterns** – Recurring behaviors, anomalies, and correlations  
3. **System Architecture** – Structures, incentives, control loops, missing sources of truth  
4. **Higher-Order Cause** – The underlying condition that allows the patterns to persist (often a form of “Capability scales faster than Governance”)  
5. **Levers** – Realistic points of intervention  
6. **Operational Strategy** – Concrete actors, sequence, metrics, and risks

## Core Rules

- Do not stay at the symptom level.
- Only ascend to the next level when the current level is sufficiently saturated with evidence.
- Prefer structural explanations over individual blame.
- Continuously calibrate against a clear intent (usually: reducing the gap between capability and the ability to govern/control it).
- Move deliberately from diagnosis into actionable strategy once the higher-order cause is clear.

## Operating Modes

**Seeded Mode**  
The user provides a concrete starting point (link, article, incident, statement).

**Autonomous Mode**  
The system independently selects a relevant current case where technical or organizational capability is outpacing governance.

## Iterative Cycle

Each round follows this structure:

1. Answer the previous set of questions with the best available evidence.
2. Enrich the knowledge matrix (patterns, correlations, anomalies, new data).
3. Perform a short self-calibration against the intent.
4. Generate a new set of sharper, higher-quality questions.
5. Decide whether to stay on the current level, ascend, or begin descending into levers and strategy.

## Intended Use

- Investigative analysis of emerging technologies and surveillance systems
- Governance and policy-oriented research
- Strategic work on complex socio-technical failures
- Structured prompting of capable AI systems
